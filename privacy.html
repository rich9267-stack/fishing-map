🎣 Fishing Map — Privacy

Last updated October 18, 2026 (coin and race corrections by the owner and administrators)

Fishing Map is a small website where a group of anglers shares fishing spots and logs catches. Anyone can ask for an account, but the organizer (Richard) approves each person, so only approved members can see anything. It is not a business and has no ads.

What we collect

  • Account: your email address and the name you choose. If you sign in with Google, we receive only your name and email address from Google.
  • Your profile: a username (handle), a display name, a "home area", and optionally a short bio, picture and tagline. The home area can be filled in for you from the home waters you pick.
  • What you add: spots, catches, notes, trips, and photos you choose to upload. Also messages you send, comments, and anything you choose to save in Settings (for example a fishing license type and its expiry date).
  • Location: only when you tap a button that uses it (such as "Where I am", "Near me", Boat mode, or the quick-catch camera), to place a spot or match a catch to a spot. If you use the safety check-in, your last position from Boat mode is shared with the buddies you chose, only if you are late.
  • Phone alerts (optional): if you turn them on, your phone's push-notification address is stored so alerts can reach it.

Who can see what

  • Every approved member can see your username, display name and home area (that is how people find and add friends). Your profile page can be limited so only friends see more.
  • Every spot and catch is Private (only you), Friends or Public (all approved members). Private spots, and their catches and photos, are visible only to you.
  • Exact locations: you and your friends see the exact pin, unless you tick "hide the exact spot from friends". Other members see a public spot only as an approximate pin (blurred by roughly 100–400 meters, up to ¼ mile), and its notes and access details stay hidden. The exception is a spot at a known public place (an official boat ramp, park or pier): it shows that place's own location, which is public information, not your exact pin.
  • Messages are visible only to the people in that conversation. You can report or block someone from the app.
  • The organizer runs the database, so can technically reach stored data; it is used only to run and protect the site.
  • To fix cheating or mistakes, the owner and chosen administrators can add correcting entries to coins and race results, always with a logged reason.
  • We don't sell or share your information with anyone else, and we don't use it for advertising.
  • Photos are shrunk on your phone before upload; the uploaded copy doesn't keep the hidden details (like GPS) that phones store inside photo files.
  • Fish ID (optional): when you tap "🔍 Identify fish", that one photo — without its location or other hidden details — is sent to Google's Gemini AI to suggest a species. This uses Google's free service, so Google may use the photo to improve its products. Nothing is sent unless you tap the button.

Where it's stored

Data is stored with Supabase (database, sign-in and photo storage). The website itself is hosted on GitHub Pages. Weather, tide, and map information is fetched from public sources (NOAA, the National Weather Service, USGS, Open-Meteo, OpenStreetMap, the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC) for red tide samples, reefs, boat ramps and depth lines, the Florida Department of Environmental Protection (FDEP) for parks, piers and jetties, and iNaturalist for public fish sightings near spot areas); these services may see your device's internet address when your browser loads their data.

Data credits

Boat ramps: Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC), Florida Boat Ramp Inventory (2009 survey, maintained by FWC). Parks, piers and jetties: Florida Department of Environmental Protection (FDEP), Division of Recreation and Parks, Florida Outdoor Recreation Inventory. These places are shown for general reference only, not for navigation. They may be out of date or closed, so check before you go. FWC and FDEP have not reviewed or endorsed this app.

Deleting your information

You can delete your own catches, spots and photos in the app. To remove your whole account and everything you've added, use Settings → Your data → Delete my account (it offers to download your catches and spots first). If you can't use the app, contact the organizer, Richard, and it will be deleted.
